# Trailer scenario

Main menu → **TRAILER SCENARIO** loads `Scenario/Trailer/baseline.json`: a separate copy of the Continue checkpoint saved on 2026-09-13 at 16:10:41 local time. It is not replaced when the campaign next autosaves. Both manual campaign saving and autosaving are blocked inside trailer sessions. Continue still loads the ordinary campaign.

After the baseline restores, the world waits paused:

The trailer carrier is **Dune Strider**, using **camo pattern**, **STEEL GREY** primary and **CREAM** secondary through the normal shared friendly-fleet livery system. Trailer enemies use black primary paint, crimson secondary paint and the skull insignia, including later vehicle/aircraft spawns. Saved normal-campaign faction assignments are unchanged.

The commander starts holding her coffee cup. Press **B** from any trailer filming camera to make her sip, then return to holding the cup. While paused, B queues the sip for the next unpause/recording; it does not start the simulation. Repeated presses during a sip do not restart it. Normal aircraft cockpit controls are unchanged.

- **Space** starts/resumes or pauses the world and event clock. **F6** remains an alias. Close the pause menu first.
- **AltGr (right Alt)** starts direct video and unpauses the action. Press it again to stop video and immediately pause the action while the MP4 encodes. Failed starts leave the pause state unchanged. Left Alt/Ctrl+left Alt do not trigger recording.
- **Left / Right arrows** select the previous / next vehicle, including both teams and the carrier. **Up / Down arrows** cycle Camera 1, Camera 2, Camera 3, Free Cam, Free Cam Anchored, plus **Cockpit / Pilot** on controllable aircraft. Both lists wrap.
- **F7** or **Pause → Restart Scenario** reloads the trailer baseline and rereads the timeline, retaining camera presets. Stop recording, wait for video encoding to finish, and leave replay first. Save important takes before restarting. Both reset routes restore the 20-second launch delay and wait paused.
- **Ctrl+F9** captures a take using the existing recording system. You can start capture while the trailer is waiting, then use F6 to begin the action.
- **Pause → Recording Mode** handles replay, camera attachment, scrubbing, and shot export as before. The trailer's status overlay is hidden in Recording Mode.

Controller filming shortcuts: **A** toggles anchoring for the current camera, **X** selects the next vehicle, **Y** selects the next camera, and **B** starts/stops direct video with the same unpause/pause coupling as AltGr. **D-pad Up/Down** doubles/halves maximum camera translation speed per press, from **0.625 to 320 m/s** (default **10 m/s**). The setting follows you between cameras and vehicles, works paused or running, and is shown in the camera overlay. Stick deflection still gives fine control; D-pad Left/Right still rolls. Existing Shift/Ctrl modifiers remain within those speed limits. Normal cockpit controls are unaffected.

Anchoring can be enabled on Camera 1–3 or either free view. It locks aim onto the pilot/commander **without changing how the camera moves with the vehicle**: Camera 1–3 retain their vehicle-relative position and follow its movement/rotation; free views remain world-positioned. Toggling it off preserves the current shot and restores manual aim. Mounted presets remember their anchor selection. The existing Free Cam Anchored slot remains as a shortcut. The camera overlay shows ANCHOR ON/OFF.

Unanchored mounted-camera movement, smoothing and yaw are integrated in the vehicle's frame, then composed with its interpolated transform. Turning/banking therefore does not push back against the operator's camera adjustments. Free and anchored views keep their world-space behavior.

- **Free Cam (view 4):** opens from the current composition and detaches from vehicle motion. Fly and aim independently. The world pose survives floating-origin shifts, and the view remains usable if the former subject disappears.

The vehicle does not carry the camera along: it can approach, pass and fly away from the lens. Movement translates the camera; mouse/right-stick aiming deliberately orbits the focus only while you give input. The seated head offset is sampled on entry, so head animation does not shake the camera. Ground vehicles without a character anchor use their body centre. Axial roll and world position survive floating-origin shifts.

Waits in Camera 1 until a live bomb from the selected aircraft is available, then follows 8 m behind its velocity vector, looking at a periodically updated ballistic impact prediction. It does not drop bombs, alter their trajectory, change damage, or start/stop video recording.

Cinematic movement, D-pad roll, and trigger zoom are disabled; those inputs use their ordinary gameplay bindings instead. Space and AltGr still pause/record, and the arrows still change vehicle/view. Switching to a filming view returns control to AI. Friendly and enemy aircraft can be piloted without changing their team; aircraft locked during deck handling cannot be taken over yet. Ground vehicles and the carrier retain the five filming views.

## Authoring the experiment

Edit `Scenario/Trailer/timeline.json`, then restart with F7. Events are ordered by `time` in simulation seconds, with unique `id` values. Equal-time events execute in file order. The director preloads the listed scenes before arming; instantiation and AI setup still have a runtime cost.

The included experiment stages the requested ground battle before F6:

- Four friendly light vehicles in platoon **Ember**, 3 km northwest of the carrier (bearing 320°).
- Eight enemies: three buggies, three pickups, and two battle buses, 2 km from Ember at bearing 285° from the friendly starting point.
- Both platoons are ordered to attack each other. Their target markers follow their living vehicles rather than staying at the spawn points.
- Dune Strider follows its saved driving route immediately on unpause. After **20 simulation seconds**, two **Aircraft 5s** with the mixed `strike` loadout (gun, bomb rack, and rocket pod on each aircraft) are queued for retrieval and launch, then join **Archer** on a ground-attack mission. Actual takeoff follows normal elevator/deck preparation. Pausing also pauses this delay; ground forces still engage from the start.

The `startup` section authors this layout. These bearings are geographic (north = world −Z, west = world −X), unlike event `spawn` positions below. The selected 2 km corridor was sampled across a 300 m width: approximately 3 m of terrain relief, with a 2,095 m navigation route. Startup checks the terrain and route and stops with an error if the layout is no longer suitable. Vehicles are placed in spaced two-column formations on the terrain. This is a sampled terrain/path check, not a guarantee against every rock or combat collision.

Supported actions:

- `cue`: show/log a beat, with `text`.
- `spawn`: `scene`, `kind` (`aircraft` or `ground`), `team` (1 friendly / 2 enemy), `position` [x, y, z], `heading_deg`, and `speed_mps`.
- `launch`: request `count` (1–6) aircraft through normal carrier operations. Optional `aircraft_model` and `loadout_profile` select the aircraft and weapons. The **request** is timed; actual retrieval and takeoff depend on hangar stock, deck machinery, and launch clearance. Accepted counts and actual launch times are logged.

Event `spawn` positions use the carrier's **initial restored frame**, not its later moving position: +X is right, +Z is ahead. Aircraft Y is metres above the baseline deck; ground Y is clearance above terrain at the authored X/Z. Heading is degrees clockwise from +Z. Spawn coordinates remain valid across floating-origin shifts. Aircraft positions below terrain + 30 m stop the experiment with an error rather than silently moving the spawn. Generic individual `spawn` events do not validate a ground route; the startup platoon layout does.

Scheduled aircraft use normal AI; generic scheduled ground vehicles initially hold/patrol their point and retain normal combat response. The startup platoons instead have mutual attack orders. Other deployed friendly/enemy forces, enemy bases/emplacements, and hostile wind farms/guards are excluded from the in-memory checkpoint before loading; any remaining world combat actors are removed before staging. The carrier and its attached crew/equipment, hangar inventory, terrain, and neutral scenery remain. Neither the baseline JSON nor the Continue save is rewritten. Automatic air mission-board tasking, rescue dispatch, CAP/escort replenishment, and enemy strategic dispatch are disabled for trailer sessions so they do not add unscheduled reinforcements. Sensors and recovery supervision remain active. Normal campaign behavior is unchanged.

This reproduces the checkpoint and **event schedule**, not a deterministic battle simulation. Physics, local AI, weather, and player actions can diverge. Use a recorded take to film the *same completed action* from different angles. The director does not currently stage commander/pilot gestures, force damage outcomes, or implement the full planned trailer choreography.

Diagnostics print `[Trailer] READY`, `EVENT`, `SPAWN`, and actual launch completion messages to the Godot log. Invalid schedules fail visibly while paused. Restart reloads the baseline; it does not try to rewind live physics.
