# Recording mode: first cinematic replay slice

## Play normally, then film afterward

1. During gameplay, press **Ctrl+F9** to start background recording, or choose **Pause → Start Recording** (this resumes gameplay).
2. Keep flying, driving, commanding, switching cameras, and fighting normally. A small **REC** indicator shows elapsed simulation time and estimated data usage. No recording camera is created, no AI handoff occurs, and gameplay input/HUD/mouse state remain intact. Pausing the game pauses capture time.
3. Press **Ctrl+F9** again to stop, or use **Pause → Stop Recording**. The shortcut does not pause gameplay; stopping through the pause menu leaves it paused. **TAKE READY** confirms the in-memory take is retained. Pressing Ctrl+F9 again will not overwrite it.
4. Open **Pause → Recording Mode**. The completed background take opens directly in replay. Opening this editor during background capture first stops capture. Scrub to the moments you want, move/attach the camera, and build Shot A/B with **Add / Replace Key** as described below.
5. **Save Take**, then **Export Shot**. Save before clearing the take or closing the game; stopping is not an automatic disk save. **Esc** returns to paused gameplay, keeping the player-control assignment. Resume continues the live world where you left it before opening replay, not a rewound simulation.

The Ctrl+F9 default uses the `toggle_background_recording` input action. **Do not use F8: Godot reserves it for stopping editor-launched games.** The original limits still apply: up to six lifetime vehicle subjects, nearby discovery within 1.8 km of the starting camera, and 120 seconds / estimated 128 MiB. This is not an unlimited session recorder. Start with a short 10–20 second take. Hitting a limit stops background capture without pausing or handing off control. A scenario change also stops capture and retains the take in memory.

## Cinematic editor / original live-camera workflow

AI carrier launches are now included in subject discovery (`ai_aircraft` as well as `aircraft`), both at capture start and when retrieved during a take. Vehicles temporarily parented to the carrier get independent tracks, not duplicate carrier geometry. Older takes that omitted these aircraft cannot recover their missing motion; record a new take after restarting the game. `Tests/RecordingAISubjectSmoketest.gd` covers AI-only discovery, group changes, retrieval/launch motion, and saved replay visibility using the Aircraft 5 model; a focused AVI export also passes.

1. Start a scenario. Open **Pause → Recording Mode**. The world starts paused. Nearby aircraft and ground vehicles can now enter the take after recording starts, including aircraft retrieved from the hangar.
2. Choose a subject. Select **Follow position**, **Follow heading**, or **Fully attached**. **Attach** applies the selection without a camera jump. **Detached** leaves the camera in world space. **Look At** independently toggles aiming at the selected subject; **Frame** moves to a simple establishing offset.
3. Hold **right mouse** and use **WASD** to fly the camera, **Q/E** down/up, and mouse movement to aim. **Z/C** roll the camera. Adjust the speed and FOV fields. **Tab** hides the panel/cursor and captures the mouse; Tab restores controls. This first UI is keyboard/mouse-oriented.
4. **Record** starts a visual take and runs the scene. It captures the selected subject plus nearby subjects/carrier, up to six lifetime subjects. New nearby vehicles are discovered every 0.1 seconds within 1.8 km of the initial camera position; recorded subjects remain tracked after leaving that area. Removed actors retain their slot so old camera keys never point to a replacement. **Run/Pause** or Space toggles the simulation while composing. Starting live action hands a player-controlled aircraft back to AI; leaving recording mode does not silently take control back.
5. **Stop**, then **Replay**. Use the slider or left/right arrows to seek/frame-step. The live scene is hidden, paused, and has its processing suspended; script-free visual copies display the recorded state. **Live** restores the original scene at the end-of-recording state, still paused.
6. Select **Shot A**, compose a camera position at a timeline time, and **Add / Replace Key**. Repeat at another time. **Preview Shot** plays the take using that camera path. **Free Camera** releases camera-path playback. Repeat independently for **Shot B**. Changing attachment spaces between keys produces a cut, not an interpolation between incompatible coordinate systems.
7. **Save Take** preserves the visual scene, tracks, and both shots in a new versioned directory. **Open Take** selects its `take.bin`. Save before **Clear Take**; clear removes only the in-memory take, never saved files.
8. **Export Shot** saves a new version and starts a separate Godot renderer. It writes a **silent 1920×1080, 30 fps AVI** into that version's directory. The UI distinguishes scene-data recording, export in progress, completion, and failure. Do not close the export renderer before it completes. Convert the AVI to an editing/delivery format afterwards if needed.
9. **Esc** returns to the pause menu and restores the previous HUD visibility/camera where still valid.

Files go under `user://recordings`, normally `%APPDATA%/Godot/app_userdata/Land Carrier/recordings` on this machine. Every save is a fresh directory containing `world.scn` and `take.bin`; the latter includes the camera keys. Keep both together. Takes reference project assets and should be treated as local project captures, not a portable archival format across arbitrary asset revisions.

## What this version records

- Subject and visible mesh transforms at nominal 30 Hz, including nested moving parts such as gear and propellers, without replaying flight physics.
- Mesh visibility, skeleton bone poses, and mesh blend-shape weights. Subject tracks use stable IDs/weak source references.
- Subject appearance/removal and late-added mesh/skeleton parts, with backwards scrubbing and save/reload support. Appearance time is the first captured sample, not a sub-frame spawn event.
- Recorded subjects opt into the existing camera-focus exterior-detail policy while capture is running; normal visual budgeting resumes afterwards. This does not activate all player-only cockpit presentation.
- Recorded aircraft temporarily retain their pooled cockpit occupants, including their full heads, independently of the live camera. Only the dormant pilot mount is restored: cockpit cameras, instruments, and audio remain dormant. Stop/clear releases the recording override and resumes normal presentation. Existing attached passenger mounts use the same override.
- Carrier tread plates provide CPU-side instance transforms, captured from the first sample and interpolated during scrub, saved-take playback, and offline export. Recorded treads keep animating off camera; the ordinary off-camera budget resumes when capture ends. No GPU readback is used to capture plate motion.
- Sparse combat visual lifetimes: fired projectile/tracer meshes, ParticleManager-managed sparks/smoke/explosion flashes/blast waves/debris, and fixed-wing detached parts. Birth/retirement hooks capture even effects shorter than the nominal sample interval. Motion, visibility, material-override color/alpha and emission energy are sampled; playback interpolates saved values without physics, RNG, damage, or effect scripts. Each pooled activation gets separate tracks/material snapshots. Already-live registered effects are discovered when recording starts.
- A bounded static mesh/environment snapshot around the initial camera position (about 1.8 km, with intersecting large mesh bounds allowed).
- Static selection uses transformed geometry bounds, including streamed chunks whose vertices are far from their node origin. Materials are snapshotted; viewport-fed display textures become frozen images (black placeholders under headless rendering), never dangling references to live cameras. Live material/texture resources are not edited.
- Independent vehicle-relative or world-space camera keys for two shots.
- Floating-origin compensation for recorded movement.

Moving the replay camera cannot change the battle. The cinematic editor's recording camera input is isolated from the commander and FlightDirector; its live-camera Run/Record workflow still uses the existing AI handoff. Background capture does not enter that editor or request a handoff. Photo mode remains unchanged. Live cockpit head masking stays in place during background capture, while recorded mesh visibility independently includes the complete pilot for later external shots.

## Deliberate limits / next work

This is a **short operations/combat-visual replay prototype**, not the complete trailer director or an exhaustive battle replay system.

- New aircraft/ground vehicles and visual parts are captured at discovery/sample ticks. Combat meshes have separate bounds: 4096 lifetime tracks, 256 concurrently tracked meshes, and a shared estimated 128 MiB take budget. New effects must begin within 1.8 km of the initial camera (with floating-origin compensation); accepted effects stay tracked. Atomic capacity rejection produces a visible, saved omission warning. This is deliberately bounded, not every projectile across the map.
- Exterior detail uses the shared focus policy, which can take a budget-update tick to settle. Combat capture observes effects the live visual budget actually creates/shows; it does not resurrect distance-culled effects for a later camera angle. GPU-only particles/trails, decals/scorch marks, arbitrary shader-time/uniform animation, live viewport-fed instruments, whole-vehicle wreck replacement, and ejected actors are not covered yet. Managed mesh effects preserve their sampled fade/emission, but other changing material properties remain snapshots. Animated MultiMesh transforms require an explicit CPU-side provider (currently carrier treads); other instance buffers, colors, and custom data remain an initial snapshot. Full-battle destruction/seat transitions still need validation.
- Environment and static geometry are a snapshot, not an entire streamed map. Do not expect uncaptured distant terrain when flying the replay camera kilometres away.
- The recorder has a 120-second / estimated 128 MiB track-data budget and a 768-track guard. Resource/geometry memory is additional. Crew skeletons make real carrier takes substantially heavier than the stripped fixture: a live 41-second trial used roughly 67 MiB of estimated track data. This is not a measured total-heap figure. Start short; the data budget can stop capture before 120 seconds. AVI has a format size limit, so long/heavy exports should be split or moved to another export format in a later iteration.
- No audio capture/mixing, semantic combat event log, authored alarm/launch timeline, slow-motion export, or actor gesture controls yet. Replay mutes live audio and restores the prior mute state on exit. Recorded damage presentation is visual state, not restored health or a resimulated battle.
- Offline export starts another renderer process and may open its own window. It does not require the live simulation to sustain the output frame rate. The initial renderer bootstrap frame is accounted for by the exporter; revalidate frame counts if the Godot startup path changes.

Next priorities: validate a short live aircraft attack with this combined recorder, audit missing muzzle flashes/trails/wreck transitions, then add sound, timed staging cues, and the pilot/commander gestures. Compressing unchanged skeleton/transform samples would help longer takes without simply raising the memory limit. Pinned pilot skeletons, animated treads, and combat effects increase track-data usage, so the unchanged 128 MiB budget may stop takes sooner. Material/viewport snapshotting adds a one-off capture-start cost and can hitch when discovering a new display-bearing actor; staged/prewarmed capture is a future improvement.

Saved takes now use format version 4 (sparse combat lifetimes, following version 3's MultiMesh transforms and version 2's variable-length actor frames). Versions 1, 2, and 3 remain accepted; older recorder code rejects version 4 rather than silently misreading it. Replay copies/camera have physics interpolation disabled because their clock is the scrub/export timeline, not the live simulation.
