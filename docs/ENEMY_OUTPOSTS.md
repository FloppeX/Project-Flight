# Enemy observation outposts

The first outpost prototype uses `Models/building - enemy outpost.glb` directly through `Buildings/building_enemy_outpost.tscn`. The GLB and its hierarchy are preserved. The separate authored radar rotates around its own pivot; both meshes provide collision on the damageable building body.

## Gameplay

- New normal regions attempt to place six compounds across the map: three columns and two rows, with randomized locations within each sector. These are in addition to the separate main enemy base, at least 6 km from the starting carrier and one another, and 3 km from the airfield. Each contains an observation building, a vehicle bay, and three gun emplacements. Placement requires a nearby navigation anchor and separate stable pads for every building: a 20 m observation pad, 28 m bay pad, and 12 m gun pads, each with no more than 1.5 m of terrain variation. Invalid sites are skipped with a warning rather than placed floating or inside cliffs.
- Each station scans every 3 seconds within 4 km. Terrain samples every 20 m between a 12 m observation point and a point 2 m above the target block sightings behind hills. This is terrain visibility, not a physics ray through every tree or building; there is no weather or night penalty yet.
- Reports take 15 seconds and retain the original sighting position. Subsequent command responses use the existing decaying contact picture. The enemy still has mobile aircraft and vehicle observers outside station coverage.
- Each station supports one four-vehicle patrol, funded from the parent base's vehicle reserve, on a roughly 1.2 km patrol route. A surviving patrol occupies its slot even if damaged or assigned to an attack. If eliminated, replacement waits approximately 120 seconds and requires four reserve vehicles.
- The vehicle bay takes 600 damage independently and swaps to its authored destroyed GLB with ruined-silhouette collision. Losing the bay stops replacement patrols, but observation, guns, and surviving patrol orders remain active. The map indicates the lost bay. Both the station and bay must survive to support replacements. Patrol deployment opens the authored bay door upward into the lintel in two seconds; it stays open for roughly eight seconds and closes in two. The shutter retracts into the roof without protruding through it, and its collision follows the opening. Door progress and remaining hold time persist in checkpoints. Vehicles still use the existing virtual deployment system; visible driving through the door is not implemented.
- In regions with stations, these local vehicle patrols replace the former 75-percent ground deployment quota. Main-base fixed-wing deployment and existing emplacements are unchanged. This substantially reduces the initial ground force and requires balance playtesting.
- An idle local patrol can respond to reports within 6 km of its home, including stations outside the airfield's normal response radius. Central command still permits only one ground response per base at a time.
- At 600 damage, the station stops observing and replacing patrols, cancels its unsent reports, and emits an asset-loss incident at its own position. Surviving units keep their orders, and reports already transmitted expire normally. The authored `building - enemy outpost destroyed.glb` replaces the intact model, with collision rebuilt for the ruined silhouette. Destroyed checkpoints load that same model.
- Explored stations appear in the ENEMY OBSERVATION map overlay described below. Destroyed stations remain marked OFFLINE with their lost capabilities.

## Observation overlay and Citadel reports

The tactical map and overview have an ENEMY OBSERVATION toggle. Red shading estimates fixed outpost visibility for a carrier on the terrain surface, using `EnemyOutpost.can_observe` and the current carrier's approximate height above ground. Terrain creates observation shadows. Coverage is sampled into a cached 48-by-48 texture per known station, built incrementally with a 2 ms work budget per frame; the overview reuses those textures. The legend explicitly says when coverage is still calculating. Terrain rebakes and changes in carrier ground offset invalidate the cache; local coverage coordinates follow origin shifts.

Faint hatching marks a broad estimated patrol operating envelope (7 km, based on the helicopter patrol radius plus observation range). It is not a current position or a guarantee of detection. It remains after a compound is disabled because deployed patrols can survive. Only explored outposts contribute coverage; the existing fog layer covers unexplored terrain. Unshaded terrain is not guaranteed safe. Hovering the map reports fixed observation, possible patrols, unknown terrain, or no known coverage.

Click an explored outpost while no mission target is being drafted to inspect observation, replacement support, and nearby operational guns. The capability display explains the effect of destroying the observation building, bay, and guns. Inspection does not issue orders or overwrite a staged mission. Destroying the observation building removes its red area immediately; bay loss removes replacement support but preserves fixed observation.

Citadel now gives radio captions for an actual pending carrier report, confirmed delivery, and an interrupted report. Warnings are rate-limited and repeat sightings do not restart the reporting deadline. The new phrases have no dedicated recorded voice clips; the existing optional radio TTS can read them. These are a deliberate player-facing intelligence aid, not a new simulated radio-interception sensor.

Carrier reports retain a transmission window when patrols materialize: ground patrols use 40-90 seconds, fixed-wing patrols 25-75 seconds, outpost helicopters 15 seconds, and stations their configured 15 seconds. Active patrols now service their pending timers. Eliminated groups cannot transmit outstanding reports. Leaving observation range alone does not cancel an already queued report. Citadel confirms successful transmission without claiming that the patrol escaped or that the reported position remains current.

The overlay's LAST REPORTED CARRIER POSITION marker follows the delivered intelligence, not the moving carrier. It expires with the existing three-minute carrier contact lifetime, follows origin shifts, and is restored through the existing saved intel state. Radio warning bookkeeping is transient and clears on region/load reset; pending reports themselves retain their existing persistence.

## Shared turret accuracy

Friendly and enemy emplacements, vehicle guns, carrier guns, and aircraft turrets use the shared controller's burst accuracy model. Each firing opportunity starts with a random sideways/vertical angular bias, held through each burst while the gun continues tracking target movement. Between bursts, the bias contracts toward a nonzero crew-skill limit. Physical rounds and cosmetic filler tracers use the barrel direction without adding fresh per-round spread. Both factions use the same aiming path. Default friendly crew skill is 0.8 versus 0.35 for enemies; explicit unit skill overrides still apply.

The initial error is 1.8 times the crew's base angular error and the settled error is 0.8 times that base. Settling takes 12 seconds for an unskilled crew or 5 seconds at maximum skill (6.4 seconds for the default friendly crew and 9.55 seconds for the default enemy crew); corrections become visible between bursts. Authored anti-air and darkness penalties still apply. These values are exposed under Burst Accuracy on the controller.

Normal pauses between bursts retain aim progress. Losing target visibility, range, alignment, firing arc, host-plane clearance, ammunition, or defense capability clears it, including during a pause. Changing or losing the target also resets it. Reacquisition starts with a fresh initial bias. Visibility checks use the actual target position so a deliberately inaccurate aim ray cannot pass beside cover and count as seeing the target.

Verified 2026-09-26: `Tests/EnemyBurstAccuracySmoketest.gd` covers coherent bursts, progressive correction, skill floors, resets in both firing and pause states, physical and cosmetic round directions, real cover/reacquisition, and friendly/enemy production turret scenes. Run it again with `-- --friendly` to exercise the full behavior and reset checks for friendly crews; equal-skill crews are also checked for identical behavior. Shared-gunner, barrel-orientation (360 cases), tracer, and projectile-collision (61 checks) regressions passed. Headless shutdown still reports existing ObjectDB leaks. In-flight difficulty and visual feel remain playtest questions.

## Persistence and integration

### Helicopter patrols

Each intact outpost supports one flight of two or three helicopters. New and replacement flights contain both Aircraft 13 and Aircraft 15; an optional third helicopter is randomly chosen from those two, and slot order is shuffled. Their authored rockets and guns are retained. Existing saved flights keep their stored airframes; new deployments use the mix. They patrol roughly 3 km around their home at 30 m/s and 140 m terrain clearance. Nearby helicopters use the existing physical helicopter controller and offset patrol routes; distant flights use the shared enemy virtualization system. This is an airborne patrol implementation: there are no physical parking pads, takeoffs, landings, or rearming at outposts yet.

Helicopters observe surface contacts within 4 km with terrain occlusion, report sightings through the existing intel system, and investigate ground/carrier reports within 8 km of home. Their strategic task remains hunting surface units; local helicopter encounters use the mission rules below. Air contacts do not dispatch them as fixed-wing interceptors. Patrol routes do not send enemy helicopters into the friendly carrier recovery loop.

Surface scanning accepts units without an `is_destroyed` flag and excludes those explicitly flagged destroyed. The legacy `AIPilot` node retained by helicopter scenes refuses fixed-wing initialization and launch requests. Carrier recovery excludes hostile aircraft, and both catapult launch entry points reject helicopters. The helicopter smoke test exercises these ownership boundaries alongside ordinary patrol behavior.

### Local aircraft threats and mission priorities

All physical helicopter pilots observe hostile aircraft within 2.5 km using canopy visibility, terrain and physics line of sight. Brief shoulder checks can reacquire a previously seen contact. Unknown aircraft behind the pilot remain unknown; projectile damage can trigger a defensive break without revealing the shooter. An observed aircraft pointing toward the helicopter within 1 km can trigger a three-second lateral break with a five-second cooldown. The pilot validates the escape chord against terrain, retains its mission destination, and resumes ordinary navigation afterward. These breaks apply during transit, not deck operations or final landing.

Outbound outpost patrols and attack-role helicopters may attack a locally observed hostile helicopter. Explicit attack orders constrain the target. Rescue, passenger carriage and return flights do not chase; rescue remains defensive even if the inspector's outbound-only combat restriction is disabled. Gun weapons are preferred against helicopters, with rockets as fallback and no bombs. Airborne rocket aiming uses a moving-target ballistic sight instead of the ground-impact pipper. Lost sight, a 60-second engagement limit or travel more than 2 km from the engagement origin ends a chase and restores the previous destination. Ground-target doctrine remains rockets first. Starting rescue or evasion cancels unsent rocket rounds; already airborne projectiles continue.

`Tests/HelicopterAirBehaviorSmoketest.tscn` covers awareness, terrain concealment, blind rear, friendly filtering, weapon preference, order preservation, rescue protection, origin shifts and attack abort. The attack diagnostic accepts `--air-target` for a moving airborne target and `--rescue` for an en-route rescue under threat. In the 2026-09-26 headless runs, the gun helicopter hit the moving target, Aircraft 13 fired two rocket volleys but missed, and the rescue run made three defensive breaks without selecting a target or firing. The surface-attack regression still hit its target. Rocket interception accuracy, formation appearance, and complex-terrain combat need playtesting.

A surviving flight retains its outpost slot, including after its home is destroyed. An eliminated flight can be replaced after approximately 240 seconds while both the station and vehicle bay survive. These local replacements have their own timer and do not consume the main base's fixed-wing reserve; finite helicopter stock and resupply are not modeled. Flight type, outpost ownership, aircraft combat state, and replacement timers persist in checkpoints. Existing saves with intact outposts gain flights through ordinary deployment evaluation.
